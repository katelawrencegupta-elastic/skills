# Canvas and PDF layout

Open this only when changing a canvas or `render_pdf.py`. The two stay in the same order. PDFs are not screenshots of the canvas.

PDFs are rendered by `render_pdf.py` from `account.json` (inline SVG, Letter landscape, Chrome `--print-to-pdf`; `CHROME_PATH` overrides).

Account section order starts with the snapshot and the Current ECH architecture table on the same page when the tallest region has at most 12 deployments. The table lists each deployment with its Elastic version, region, classified solution, availability zones, lookback ECU, utilization (share of lookback ECU), stored size, document count, and average document size. A single-zone row stays highlighted. The topology is the next page: a diagram of provider, region, and availability-zone groups, then each component as RAM × node count. That count is the zone count on the Cloud SKU: ECH places one node in each zone, and extra autoscaled nodes are not a separate field. A taller fleet keeps the architecture table on its own page and shows the first 12 deployments in each region on the topology page. Then 1 platform (zone, billing, and instance tables in shaded boxes, with space between the boxes), 2 versions & ECU, **3 burndown and 4 deployments over time on one page** (each chart and the balance table in its own panel; the balance panel adds the 3- and 6-month projection at the trailing-year complete-month pace), **5 storage on its own page** (stored GB over time) and **6 ECU by solution on its own page** (monthly ECU as a percent of the commit), **7 ML on its own page**, and 8 Current State Analysis & Recommendations on its own page with the action, the impact, and the Elastic docs that apply. **§9 cost recs and §10 sizing move onto the consolidation-savings PDF** when that report runs (ECH > 50 %); they stay on the account PDF otherwise. A deployment billed in 1 availability zone raises a warning on §1 and a performance recommendation. Long display tables are capped (top 4–6). `account.json` keeps the full cloud, storage, and AI deployment lists.

## §11 Serverless consolidation savings

This section belongs to `diag-serverless-consolidation`. The account-summary skill does not build it.

`<slug>-serverless-consolidation-savings.canvas.tsx` is the quoted consolidation. By default that is bulk ingest and measured retention from the diagnostic bundles (`brief.ingest_source` = `diagnostics`). The diagnostic run writes two PDFs from that bulk: `<key>_full retention_bulk_serverless_consolidation_plan` at the measured month rounded up, and `<key>_30d_bulk_serverless_consolidation_plan` at month 1. Each links to its own sheet. All four estimate sheets are in the Drive child `generated_estimates`. Deployment-plan sheets are in `generated_estimates/plan_sheets`. Measured-retention PDFs are in `full_retention`. 30-day PDFs are in `30d`. The account PDF stays in the customer Drive folder. `<slug>-serverless-consolidation-from-k360.canvas.tsx` exists only when `brief.k360_ingest` is set: stored GB ÷ assumed retention. K360 does not provide a raw ingest total, so that backup can sit far below measured bulk. Build each from its own brief block (`savings` / `quotes` / `notes`, or `k360_ingest.savings` / `k360_ingest.quotes` / `k360_ingest.notes`). Do not mix the quotes. The backup PDF is `<key>_Serverless_consolidation_from_k360.pdf`. The backup does not get the 30-day pass.

Own canvas (`<slug>-serverless-consolidation-savings.canvas.tsx`), ECH > 50 % only, built from `*_brief.json`.

When a contract rate exists, headline Stats compare ECH at ARR ÷ (commit ECU/day × 365) with serverless at estimator list. The $1/ECU list view is omitted. With no ARR or commit, the comparison stays at $1/ECU. The PDF cover is the last 30 days of billed ECU unless `--lookback-days` says otherwise. The page-1 chart title, both bar labels, and the legend say that window. ECH is that total ÷ the number of days × 30.416 at the contract rate. The estimators are filled at that same pace, and `savings` is their read-back. What if data is kept longer, or the fleet is busier shows that window first, then the complete-month average and the quietest month as scales of the read-back. The deployment table divides lookback ECU by lookback days ÷ 30.416. Those two figures do not add up. A single-region year can be the sheet's year-1 cell. The savings annual is 12 × the monthly read-back. The body then flows on as few pages as the content needs, and the headings are not numbered: Where ECH spend sits (the top deployments, the callouts, and the column notes together) → What you pay for today, and what replaces it, without that deployment table → the active deployments' overhead share → data egress (data in, data out, and inter-node: lookback ECU, ECU/mo, and contract $ when a rate exists; data out carries over outside the estimator at 50 GB free then $0.05/GB for Observability and Security; inter-node does not carry over, and on Azure it is already waived; do not convert ECU to GB) → One project per solution in each region → What if data is kept longer, or the fleet is busier (stored GB ÷ months, which is not the bulk rate and does not reprice a diagnostic quote, plus fleet at the contract rate and the scaled serverless estimate; the usage chart is full width) → Cost-savings analysis → Reconciliation and what to confirm before quoting, immediately before the Appendix. The Appendix is last and starts on its own page. It holds the input arithmetic, the metered-rate tables, the search-power chart when Search was estimated, and the quote integrity score. Page 1 puts the percent reduction in its own box beside the ECH cost, the serverless estimate, and the dollar reduction. The ECH-versus-serverless chart on that page labels the active deployment count and the serverless project count. The next page puts horizontal Observability deployment-count bars down the left column, each row labeled with the project count and the monthly dollars, and the component totals and sizing inputs on the right. The 100% bar reuses the headline Observability total. Search-power profiles are in the Appendix only when Search was estimated, and they are not added to D32 or E74. Quote D32 only when unformatted G26 is within 0.51 GB/day of the bulk written. Quote E74 only when E73 is 0.

On the deployment-count page the component tables are the right column. Sizing inputs keeps Solution, Deployments, ECU/mo, Stored GB, Kept (mo), and GB/day on one row. When there is no deployment-count chart, those tables stack at full width. What you pay for today puts the ECH-versus-serverless comparison and the monthly build-up chart in two side-by-side panels. The projects table is a full-width row under that pair, not inside the comparison panel. Where ECH spend sits keeps the top-deployment table, the callouts, and the column notes together. A row whose fixed overhead is above 50% of its ECU is shaded, with a caption. Reconciliation is the last section before the Appendix. It keeps the estimator read-backs and the before-you-quote notes together; the notes run in two columns. The Appendix starts on its own page after that section and holds the input arithmetic, the metered-rate tables, the search-power chart when Search was estimated, and the quote integrity score. Tables use the same cell padding above and below, whether they sit in a column or on their own. Consolidation type is larger than the account report, with more space between the heading, the table, the notes, and the chart. Bar charts are drawn with a top face and a side face.

When a solution spans regions, each project gets its own sizing named by region, and the report links each one. The headline is the sum of those read-backs. Search $ is the es3-estimator on-demand read-back for that region. When Search deployments bill jina embeddings, the ML-node ECU on those deployments is added to the EIS line at list, along with the billed inference tokens. ML nodes on deployments with no jina SKU stay out of that line. Observability and Security $ are the sheet read-backs. The contract rate is applied to ECH only. Observability and Security tokens stay on the §7 meter and are not added to Search.
